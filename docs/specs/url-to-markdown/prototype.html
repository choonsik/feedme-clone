Use the saw, fear the saw

Steph Ango

When I learned to use a table saw, my teacher impressed upon me that the machine wants to cut fingers. Fear the saw!

Power tools reward respect, not bravado. The people who get hurt are rarely beginners, because beginners are still afraid. It is the person with ten years of experience who gets comfortable, reaches over the blade, and stops paying attention.

Habits that keep you safe

  • Never stand directly behind the workpiece.
  • Use a push stick for every narrow cut, even the quick ones.
  • Switch the saw off and wait for the blade to stop before you clear scraps.
Fear is not the opposite of skill. It is the part of skill that keeps your hands attached.

The same idea applies far beyond the workshop. Any tool that gives you leverage, from a table saw to a production database, deserves a short checklist you run every time:

1. What can this do to me?
2. What is between me and that?
3. Would I notice if it went wrong?
